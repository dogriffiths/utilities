The three hypotheses

What explains the account?

H1

Authentic substitution

In late July 1941 Kolbe volunteered for Franciszek Gajowniczek. Memory variance and pious embellishment gathered around a real event.

H2

Postwar legend

The story was invented or retrofitted after 1945 onto a priest who was in fact selected arbitrarily.

H3

Wartime rumour

A camp rumour conflated unrelated prisoners. Something was said inside the camp, but not an exchange between these two men.

H1 allows embellishment. It claims the core exchange is real, not that every detail in later retellings is.

Soft spots

  • The ratings are my reading of the supplied research. I did not re-check the archives.
  • E4 and E5 draw on witnesses gathered largely by Conventual Franciscans, so devotional bias is possible.
  • The clerk and other secular witnesses spoke decades later, in 1968–1978, so memory drift is possible.
  • Pilecki heard the story secondhand and names no one, so E1 alone doesn't prove the priest was Kolbe.
  • E2 and E4 overlap, because both rest on people standing on the roll-call square.

Holds firm

  • Pilecki's report is dated 1943, before any sainthood campaign
  • State prosecutors, not the Church, recorded the clerk's testimony
  • SS records place both men and the dates
H1 stays first without the Franciscan-collected items. Dropping E4 and E5 leaves H1 on 0, H3 on 3 and H2 on 6.

What H1 assumes

What H2 and H3 need

A story already told in 1943, and secular prosecutors who confirmed it

Missing German paper is expected at Auschwitz, so it barely separates the hypotheses. The testimony does. Even discounting every Franciscan-collected witness, H1 stays first.

Limits

What would change the ranking

ACH ranks hypotheses by how little evidence contradicts them. It measures relative plausibility, not probability.

Established: Kolbe volunteered for Gajowniczek and died on 14 August 1941. Not established: the exact day (29 July to early August), the escapee's identity, the hostage count, the words exchanged, and whether anyone saw the phenol injection.

Later retellings add invented dialogue and scenes. Even sympathetic investigators such as Fr. Bar rejected the patriotic speech as embellishment.

Would weaken H1

  1. An SS or camp document naming a different prisoner in Gajowniczek's place
  2. Evidence that Pilecki's priest story describes a different incident
  3. Proof that witness statements were coached or drew on one source
  4. Proof that the clerk's 1975 testimony was shaped by earlier devotional literature
