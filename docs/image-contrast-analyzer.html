How these are computed. Each pixel's luminance is taken as L = 0.2126·R + 0.7152·G + 0.0722·B (perceptual weighting), scaled 0–255. All metrics below reflect whatever adjustments are currently applied.

Brightness is the mean luminance across all pixels.

RMS contrast is the standard deviation of the normalized luminance (0–1 scale) — a global measure of intensity variation that works well for complex natural images.

Michelson contrast is (Lmax − Lmin) / (Lmax + Lmin) — best suited to simple stimuli with a clear peak and trough, and can be dominated by single outlier pixels in photos.

Weber contrast is traditionally (Ifeature − Ibackground) / Ibackground for a small feature on a uniform background. Since a general photo has no single "background," this tool shows two approximations using the mean luminance as the background estimate: contrast of the brightest region and of the darkest region relative to the mean.

Red/amber/green bands are general-purpose rules of thumb (very under/overexposed or very flat/harsh vs. a comfortable mid-range) — not a universal or clinical standard, and "correct" exposure/contrast is often intentional (e.g. low-key photography).

Auto Levels stretches each of the R, G and B channels independently so its darkest pixel becomes 0 and its brightest becomes 255, then your Brightness/Contrast sliders apply on top of that.
